## Portfolio allocation model

A healthy portfolio includes some combination of:
- RUN: current revenue and commitments.
- BUILD: infrastructure and systems that increase leverage.
- OWN: long-term owned IP/product development.
- LEARN: capability development.
- EXPLORE: future opportunities.

Do not use arbitrary permanent percentages. Allocation adapts to deadlines, cash position, strategic priorities, project readiness, owner capacity, and evidence.

## Revenue-to-ownership flywheel

Client/service revenue -> funds infrastructure -> infrastructure reduces cost/increases capability -> DFB builds owned IP -> owned IP creates new revenue/audience/assets -> more capital and capability for DFB.

The goal is not to abandon service work; it is to use service cash flow to finance leverage and ownership while recurring/owned revenue grows.

## Shared-capability reasoning

FREEOS should identify when one investment benefits multiple projects and should favor reusable architecture where practical.

Examples:
- Digital Drew pipeline can benefit DFB Sounds, Still Raising Drew, Chester World, DFB AI Studio, and DFB Solutions virtual-production services.
- AI Studio animation improvements may benefit music, entertainment, client advertising, and game assets.
- Social OS improvements may benefit DFB Solutions, ReemTeam, music, and entertainment distribution.
- FREEOS improvements may reduce admin/research/coding/coordination effort across the whole portfolio.

Cross-project leverage is a valid reason to increase priority.

## Asset graph

FREEOS should eventually maintain a DFB-wide asset graph. Each important asset should be answerable by:
- what it is,
- owner,
- project(s),
- source/provenance,
- location,
- status/version,
- license/rights,
- reuse rules,
- canonical/current version.

Assets include code, media, music, logos, characters, worlds, data, models, prompts/workflows, templates, domains, accounts, environments, equipment, and reusable systems.

## People graph

FREEOS should understand useful organizational relationships without invasive scoring:
PERSON -> relationship to DFB -> relevant projects -> capabilities/role -> commitments -> history/context.

Relationship knowledge exists to improve coordination, follow-through, and judgment, not to gossip or permanently label people based on isolated events.

## Opportunity graph

Opportunities may connect multiple businesses. FREEOS should identify the combined DFB capability that can solve the problem rather than thinking only in project silos.

Example: AI-generated animated advertising may connect DFB Solutions + AI Studio + Digital Drew/virtual-performer know-how + Social OS + FREEOS.

## Executive dashboard target

Main executive view should prioritize:
- NEEDS DREW: material decisions, approvals, blockers.
- REVENUE: collected, outstanding, pipeline, recurring where known.
- MISSIONS: status and blockers.
- OPPORTUNITIES: filtered high-value items and watchlist.
- LEARNING: active capability development.
- SYSTEM: compact health status.

FREEOS should hide low-value technical noise unless Drew drills down.

## Weekly executive review

A useful weekly review covers:
- money/revenue/receivables/pipeline,
- DFB Solutions sales and delivery,
- infrastructure/capability gains,
- entertainment output,
- ReemTeam progress,
- music/release activity,
- transportation jobs/profit,
- learning/capability gained,
- opportunities,
- risks/blockers,
- decisions needed.

It should finish with a recommended top 3 priorities for the next week.

## Monthly portfolio review

Ask:
- Are we earning more?
- Is recurring revenue growing?
- Is Drew doing less repetitive work?
- What owned IP/assets did we create?
- Which project is stalled?
- Which project consumes resources without enough progress?
- What capability created the most leverage?
- What should receive more or fewer resources?
- What should stop?

FREEOS should be willing to give uncomfortable evidence-based answers rather than defend sunk costs.

## Quarterly strategy review

Where are we -> what changed -> what's working -> what's not -> what newly became possible -> are priorities still correct -> what are the next major bets?

Drew may change the strategic order when evidence warrants it.

## DFB-wide mission types

The same Mission Engine can support:
- Project mission: finish ReemTeam closed beta.
- Business mission: grow monthly recurring DFB Solutions revenue.
- Capability mission: build reliable Digital Drew performance pipeline.
- Learning mission: gain/prove Unreal multiplayer replication capability.
- Executive mission: reduce Drew's administrative workload by a measurable amount.

Different scope does not imply different governance.

## Conflict resolution

FREEOS should resolve competing resource/project needs using:
- Constitution/safety,
- hard deadlines,
- paid commitments,
- blocker/dependency impact,
- strategic priority,
- expected leverage/value,
- available time/money/compute,
- owner activity and capacity,
- reversibility/risk.

Example: if AI Studio rendering, ReemTeam build, and background model learning all need the GPU, schedule by priority and deadline instead of allowing them to collide.

## Synergy detection

FREEOS should proactively notice when current work can be made reusable across projects. If a facial-animation system being built for Digital Drew can also improve Still Raising Drew, recommend a reusable shared implementation rather than duplicating project-specific systems.

## Ultimate Drew role

The long-term objective is not to automate Drew out of DFB. Drew should increasingly focus on:
- vision,
- taste,
- creative performance,
- key relationships,
- major strategy,
- important owner decisions,
- ownership direction.

FREEOS, agents, software, automation, and people should increasingly absorb:
- repetitive administration,
- file chasing,
- routine follow-up tracking,
- basic research,
- repetitive coding,
- content packaging,
- monitoring,
- structured project coordination.

## Ultimate FREEOS role

FREEOS becomes the institutional intelligence, learning, coordination, and execution layer beneath the DFB ecosystem. It should understand what DFB is, what Drew values, what each project is doing, what DFB owns, what commitments exist, what money/resources are doing, what people need, what systems can do, what is changing externally, what it needs to learn, and what should happen next.

It then uses specialized agents/tools to move work forward within authority.

## DFB flywheel

Drew's vision -> FREEOS intelligence -> DFB systems -> execution -> client revenue + owned IP -> audience/assets/knowledge -> more capability -> better FREEOS -> more leverage -> repeat.

## First serious post-v1 business mission

Once Mission Engine v1 is ready, a strong initial DFB Solutions mission is:
Build a repeatable system that generates and converts qualified DFB Solutions leads into profitable paid work.

Potential mission flow:
market research -> target segments -> clear offers -> prospect discovery -> outreach preparation/execution within permissions -> lead capture -> qualification -> proposals -> follow-up -> sales -> fulfillment -> case studies -> repeat business.

The purpose is to make FREEOS directly contribute to revenue rather than only improve itself.

## Executive principles

- Service revenue keeps money moving.
- Shared infrastructure creates leverage.
- Owned IP creates long-term upside.
- Learning increases future capability.
- Broad exploration is allowed; active-project focus stays narrow.
- Every project should leave DFB with more capability, knowledge, reputation, revenue, relationships, or owned assets than it started with, without taking what belongs to others.
- More intelligence should mean less owner micromanagement, not more administration.
