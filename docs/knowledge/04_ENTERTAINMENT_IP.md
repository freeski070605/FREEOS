# DFB Entertainment IP Portfolio

Status: APPROVED / CANONICAL
Owner: Drew
Approved: 2026-10-05

Entertainment IP is DFB's #4 strategic priority within the wider portfolio. Its purpose is to build owned audience, catalog value, characters/worlds, sponsorship/licensing potential, merchandise possibilities, recurring media, and long-term brand equity rather than relying only on client revenue.

Portfolio:
- Still Raising Drew — narrative/animation IP.
- Get Ya 5 — recurring sports/gaming entertainment IP.
- Chester Game / Chester World — long-horizon interactive world/game IP.

Shared infrastructure should support all three without erasing each property's identity.

## Shared studio strategy

FREEOS: project intelligence, planning, knowledge, production tracking, asset intelligence, schedules, analytics, research, learning, and agent coordination.

DFB AI Studio: concept art, character consistency, animation, image/video generation, virtual production, environments, garments/assets, performance transfer, and visual experimentation.

DFB Social OS: clips, teasers, trailers, highlights, repurposing, platform packaging, distribution planning, and analytics feedback.

Drew Free / DFB Sounds: original music, soundtrack identity, crossover content, and artist exposure.

DFB Solutions: web/apps/infrastructure, business operations, production capability, and technical systems.

Strategic flywheel: service revenue -> shared capability -> owned IP -> audience/distribution -> monetization -> more IP.

## Still Raising Drew

### Identity
An original animated dramedy centered on Drew, Lil Drew, single Black fatherhood, humor, everyday life, struggle, joy, growth, and broader Black fatherhood experiences.

The message should emerge through story rather than lectures. Tone: funny, grounded, emotionally honest when earned. Avoid trauma-only storytelling, perfect-dad propaganda, generic sitcom writing, or preachy messaging.

Drew's character may make mistakes, be frustrated, funny, immature at times, learn, win, and remain recognizably human.

### Broader fatherhood universe
The series can include different fathers in different circumstances: married, divorced/co-parenting, young, older, stepfather, rebuilding relationships, struggling financially, successful but personally challenged, and other nuanced experiences. No one character needs to represent every Black father.

### Production ladder
1. 60–90 second Drew + Lil Drew proof-of-concept scene.
2. ~3-minute mini episode testing setup/conflict/humor/emotional turn/ending.
3. ~5-minute piece testing consistency across longer narrative.
4. 8–12 minute episodes after the pipeline proves reliable.

Do not start with a polished 22-minute episode.

### Production pipeline
Story idea -> script -> shot/performance plan -> voice/performance reference -> storyboard -> animatic -> character/environment assets -> animation -> compositing -> Premiere edit -> music/sound design -> QC -> master -> social cutdowns.

Premiere remains the primary edit hub. AI accelerates production; it does not replace storytelling judgment.

### Series bible
Maintain canonical character identities, relationships, personalities, ages, locations, backstory, visual design, speech patterns, recurring jokes, continuity, themes, timeline, and boundaries. FREEOS must distinguish canon from exploratory ideas.

### Music integration
Drew Free music may support opening/closing music, transitions, emotional sequences, animated music moments, soundtrack releases, internal-monologue sequences, and promotional crossover. Show discovery can feed music discovery and vice versa.

### Monetization ladder
Audience -> platform monetization -> sponsorships -> soundtrack/music -> merchandise -> licensing/distribution -> larger partnerships. Do not force monetization before the audience exists.

## Get Ya 5

### Identity
Get Ya 5 is sports/gaming entertainment using NBA 2K as the simulation/performance engine. It should feel like a recurring fictional professional league/media property rather than a casual 2K league.

Core concept:
- 16 teams,
- color-based team identities,
- common league court/environment,
- AI-controlled games,
- commentary,
- standings,
- highlights,
- recurring programming,
- Twitch-first live presentation with YouTube archive/highlights/episodes.

Because AI controls the games, entertainment centers on what happens in the league rather than human esports skill.

### Story and programming opportunities
Rivalries, records, rankings, playoff scenarios, storylines, predictions, awards, historical statistics, highlighted matchups, weekly recap, power rankings, Game of the Week, playoff push, and championship programming.

A recurring schedule should create viewer habit.

### Production loop
Schedule game -> stream/record -> commentary -> capture result/stats -> update standings -> recap -> highlights -> social content -> update storylines -> next-matchup promotion.

Statistics themselves should become content.

### Brand consistency
Maintain canonical league logo, small mark, banner, court identity, broadcast graphics, team color identities, standings/score design, social templates, and championship branding. Teams can remain visually simple and recognizable.

### Ownership caution
DFB may own the Get Ya 5 brand, original graphics, commentary, statistics database, original media, surrounding content, and other original IP. Underlying NBA 2K software/game assets remain owned by their rights holders. Commercial expansion must respect applicable platform/game/IP terms.

### Success metrics
Repeat viewers, watch time, subscribers/followers, community/chat participation, returning viewers, highlight performance, storyline engagement, sponsor interest. The league is working when viewers care what happens next.

### Monetization possibilities
Twitch/YouTube monetization, sponsorships, branded broadcasts, merchandise, sponsored segments, community memberships, events, and partnerships as appropriate.

## Chester Game / Chester World

### Identity
Chester World is DFB's long-horizon flagship digital-world/game IP centered on Chester and nearby communities. The reusable world can support story gameplay, open-world multiplayer concepts, music, sports, simulation, virtual production, cinematics, and other experiences.

Geographic scope includes Chester, Chester Township, Brookhaven, and an expanding direction that includes Upland.

The existing geographic/world-building pipeline is itself a valuable reusable digital asset. FREEOS should preserve the distinction between raw real-world data, refined assets, game-ready assets, placed assets, and optimized canonical assets.

### Core story structure
Prologue: protagonist roughly age 17–18, senior year, establishing relationships, neighborhood, friends, dreams, choices, and early consequences.

Main era: protagonist roughly age 22–23. Earlier choices may matter later.

Core themes include choice, consequences, loyalty, betrayal, trust, ambition, identity, friendship, family, survival, and opportunity.

### Trust economy
The world should remember meaningful behavior. Individuals, crews, neighborhoods, businesses, and organizations may respond to betrayal, robbery, loyalty, or other choices over time. Consequences should not reset merely because a mission ends.

### Life paths
Potential paths include music, basketball, football, business, street activity, legitimate work, police/law-enforcement path, and social/community relationships. The game should not force every player into one criminal storyline.

### Music and sports as systems
Music should be world infrastructure through local artists, radio, studios, performances, venues, soundtrack, possible career path, and Drew Free/DFB Sounds integration.

Sports should include meaningful basketball/football progression rather than decorative courts only.

### Multiplayer
Open-world multiplayer remains a long-term target, not phase one. World, movement, interactions, core systems, gameplay loop, missions, AI, trust, and performance must work before large-scale networking.

### Vertical-slice strategy
Do not build the whole city/game simultaneously.

Slice 1: several blocks, one interior, player traversal, traffic/pedestrian proof, one interaction system, one short mission, dialogue, basic persistent trust consequence.

Slice 2: more buildings/interiors, NPC routines, sports activity, vehicles, additional choices, persistent consequences.

Slice 3: economy, music, jobs, relationships, larger mission structure, expanded map.

The question for each slice is whether the small piece actually feels like the intended game.

### Accuracy vs gameplay
Real geography is an advantage, not a prison. Gameplay may justify compressed distances, altered interiors, combined structures, modified roads, fictional businesses, and layout changes. Aim for authenticity, not GIS literalism.

### Rights/privacy judgment
Distinguish public locations, private individuals, trademarks, businesses, sensitive real-world events, fictionalized material, and rights/privacy concerns. Do not automatically reproduce everything literally.

### World asset pipeline
Real-world data -> world database -> terrain/roads/buildings -> architectural refinement -> game-ready optimization -> materials/props/signage -> NPC/gameplay layer -> story/missions.

AI/FREEOS can assist with architecture references, environments, prop workflows, NPC dialogue drafts, mission concepts, QA, world-data processing, coding, and procedural tools, but quality/consistency outrank random asset volume.

## Entertainment asset and canon governance

Every meaningful entertainment asset should eventually track asset, project, type, owner, source, status, version, license, reuse rights, publish rights, and location.

Project knowledge should separate:
- CANON: officially true in the world/property.
- DEVELOPMENT: currently being built.
- IDEAS: possible future concepts.
- PRODUCTION: scripts/episodes/games/events.
- ASSETS: existing production material.
- AUDIENCE: performance/feedback.
- BUSINESS: monetization/licensing/sponsors.
- HISTORY: decisions and previous versions.

An idea is never automatically canon.

Each property should eventually have a canonical IP bible answering: what is this world, who lives in it, what are its rules, what tone belongs here, what happened already, and what cannot contradict existing canon.

## Audience and content flywheel

Use real audience behavior as evidence without letting analytics dictate creativity.

Every major entertainment output should create secondary content. Example: an episode can create trailer, shorts, behind-the-scenes, music clip, character posts, and discussion. A Get Ya 5 game can create highlights, recap, power rankings, memes/moments, and next-game promotion.

Cross-promotion should make sense and not blur brand identity.

## Entertainment economics

Track hours, direct cost, production cost, audience growth, finished assets, reusable technology, revenue, opportunities, and milestones. Art need not immediately pay for itself, but DFB should know where resources are going.

Potential portfolio monetization includes platform revenue, sponsorship, advertising, music, merchandise, licensing, game sales, appropriate in-game revenue, events, partnerships, and content rights.

## Priority inside entertainment

3. Chester World — continue meaningful development as the major long-horizon build.

This ordering is within entertainment only; entertainment remains #4 in the wider DFB strategic order.

## One-year direction

Still Raising Drew: recognizable series with reliable short-episode pipeline and real audience data.

Get Ya 5: functioning recurring league/media property with meaningful programming history or completed season.

Chester World: convincing playable vertical slice plus much stronger reusable digital Chester world.

Shared studio: mature enough to create all three more efficiently.

## Governing entertainment rule

Every major development cycle should move toward something another human can actually watch, play, hear, or experience. Avoid endless pipeline experiments, model tests, logo revisions, or world expansion without public/usable artifacts.

Entertainment thesis: use DFB's technology, music, creative ability, AI infrastructure, local identity, and storytelling to build entertainment properties DFB owns—and production systems that let a small operation create at a scale that normally requires a larger studio.
